# Stage 8: The VIA

The W65C22S VIA is Clementina's I/O chip. Its port A selects the extended
RAM bank, its port B goes to the user port, and its Timer 1
interrupts the CPU about 190 times a second. The kernel uses that interrupt
to blink the cursor, which makes the blink this stage's first check.

## What you add

The VIA (U3, W65C22S) on board 3, with its 100 nF capacitor, the diode D2 on its
interrupt output, and eight LEDs on port B. The VIA takes its bus lines
from its neighbours' rows, not from the CPU's: D0-D7 and A0-A3 from the
extended RAM (U2) right below it, RWB and PHI2 from U9 (74AC00). Its data
pins carry D7-D0 on the same pin numbers as the CPU's, 26 to 33. Its port A
takes over the extended RAM's bank pins from the stage 7 GND wires.

- **U3** goes on board 3 in columns 40-59, notch toward column 1, legs in
  rows d and h. Its incoming bus wires land in row a; rows b and c stay free
  for the pull-ups and a probe.
- **The bus wires** from U2 (D0-D7, A0-A3) leave U2's free holes and go
  round the chips' ends to board 3: up the gap at the VIA's left end
  (columns 38-39) or round the boards' right end. They drop into the VIA's
  top rows from above. RWB and PHI2 come from U9's bottom rows the same way.
  None of them goes over a chip.
- **Port A, one wire at a time.** Pull the GND wire from U2.3 and put a
  green wire from that hole to U3.2 (PA0), then the same for U2.31 to U3.3,
  U2.2 to U3.4, U2.30 to U3.5 and U2.1 to U3.6. They land on the VIA's
  bottom pins, the side facing board 2: U2.30 and U2.31 rise straight up;
  U2.1-3 are on U2's far side, so go round its left end.
- **D2** crosses board 2's gap at a slant: its band in column 23, row d
  (the top half, where the green wire from U3.21 lands in row b), its other
  leg in column 22, row g, the `~IRQB` strip from stage 4. The band faces
  the VIA's wire.
- **The LEDs.** Board 3 has no free strips left for a resistor and an LED
  each, so solder each LED's 1 kΩ to its long leg (or use LEDs with the
  resistor built in) and put it straight from the PB row's free hole (row i)
  to the GND rail. Long leg toward the VIA.
- **The 100 nF capacitor** goes across the rails at the VIA's right end
  (column 60), next to pin 20's red wire.
- **The optional 10 kΩ pull-ups** (dashed) go from CA1/CA2 (pins 40/39,
  top) and CB1/CB2 (pins 18/19, bottom) to the + rail.

The register selects run backwards: RS0 (A0) is pin 38 and RS3 (A3) pin 35.

On the PCB, port B and CA1, CA2, CB1 and CB2 go only to the user port
header. On the breadboard, port B drives the LEDs (through their 1 kΩ
resistors; the VIA's outputs have no current limit of their own). CA1, CA2,
CB1 and CB2 can be left open, but floating handshake inputs can pick up
noise and set interrupt flags the kernel doesn't expect. A 10 kΩ resistor
from each to 3.3 V is a cheap insurance; it is a breadboard extra, not part
of the schematic.

## Check

**Before you plug in**, run the
[power-up checks](../breadboard.md#before-you-power-up) on what you just
added.

1. Plug in, start the video client, and press RESET. The banner and
   `READY.` come up as in stage 6, and **the cursor now blinks**, about
   once every 0.8 s. The blink comes from the VIA's Timer 1 interrupt, so it
   proves the VIA, its chip select, the data bus to it, and the interrupt
   path through D2.
2. The blink is counted in clock cycles, not seconds. On the console, type
   `speed 2400000`: it blinks twice as fast. `speed 1200000` puts it back
   (so does unplugging: MIA starts at 1.2 MHz at every power-up).
3. Light the LEDs from BASIC:

   ```basic
   POKE 49154,255
   POKE 49152,85
   ```

   49154 is `$C002`, port B's direction register: 255 makes all eight pins
   outputs. 49152 is `$C000`, port B itself: 85 is `0101 0101`, so PB0, PB2,
   PB4 and PB6 light. `POKE 49152,170` lights the other four.
4. Make them count: type this program, then `RUN`.

   <!-- example: skip -->
   ```basic
   10 FOR I=0 TO 255:POKE 49152,I:NEXT:GOTO 10
   ```

   PB0 flickers fastest and PB7 slowest. Stop it with `Ctrl+C`.
5. In WozMon (`MON`), read the registers back. `C002` shows `FF`. Read
   `C004` twice: the two values differ, because Timer 1 counts down every
   clock cycle.
6. Run the stage 6 address test and the stage 7 string test again, then
   RESET: the VIA must not disturb the RAM.

### Every bank line

After power-up the VIA's port A pins float until the kernel sets them. The
kernel does that first thing at every reset, selecting bank 0, and the
loader only writes below `$8000`, so they need no pull-downs.

The kernel already set `$C003` (port A's direction) to `1F`, PA0-PA4 as
outputs, and selected bank 0 in `$C001`. In WozMon (`MON`), store a different
value at `8000` in the banks that each turn on one bank line:

```text
C001: 00
8000: 11
C001: 01
8000: 22
C001: 02
8000: 33
C001: 04
8000: 44
C001: 08
8000: 55
C001: 10
8000: 66
```

Now go back through the same six banks and read `8000` in each (`C001: 00`,
then `8000`; `C001: 01`, then `8000`; and so on). They read `11`, `22`,
`33`, `44`, `55` and `66`. If two banks show the same value, the PA line
that tells them apart is open or on the wrong AS6C4008 (U2) pin.

Press RESET to go back to BASIC, which needs bank 0 selected.

## With a scope

- **The interrupt.** Probe the VIA's U3.21 (`~VIAIRQ`) and trigger on a falling edge.
  It goes low every 5.21 ms (Timer 1 counts 6250, plus two cycles to
  reload, at 1.2 MHz) and comes back up a few microseconds later, when the
  kernel's interrupt handler reads Timer 1 and clears the flag. On the CPU's U1.4
  (`~IRQB`) the same pulse sits a diode drop higher: about 0.2-0.3 V instead
  of 0 V, still well under the CPU's threshold.
- **The chip select's setup time.** The VIA needs `~CS2` low at least about
  10 ns before PHI2 rises. Run the counting program, put channel 1 on PHI2
  (U3.25, the VIA) and channel 2 on `~IOCS0` (U3.23), and trigger on `~IOCS0`
  falling. At 1.2 MHz the select falls hundreds of nanoseconds before PHI2
  rises. Note the gap: stage 10 watches it shrink.

## If it fails

| Symptom | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| No blink, everything else fine | D2 reversed (its band must face U3.21), or `~CS2` (U3.23) not on `~IOCS0` |
| No blink, and the screen shows nothing new after RESET | The VIA holds the data bus: check it's not in backwards, and that U3.22 is RWB |
| LEDs stay dark | RS0-RS3 swapped (they run backwards), LEDs reversed, or CS1 (U3.24) not at 3.3 V |
| The wrong LEDs light | PB pins in the wrong order, or the LEDs not in PB0-PB7 order |
| `C004` reads the same twice | PHI2 not reaching U3.25 |
| One LED stays dark | That LED in backwards, or its resistor not soldered well |
| `READY.` stops appearing once the VIA is in | `~IRQB` held low: D2 in backwards (band toward U3.21) |
| Every bank shows the same value | PA0-PA4 on the wrong U2 pins: re-check U2 rows 1, 2, 3, 30 and 31, or a stage 7 GND wire still in |
| Banks pair up (`01` reads as `00`) | That one PA line is open |
| Strings break after adding the VIA | A wire knocked out of a U2 row: re-run the stage 7 string test and re-seat it |
